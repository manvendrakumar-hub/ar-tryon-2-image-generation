The customer, wearing the exact frame, in every product photo

A customer's face scan becomes their own product photos: the frame they are looking at, on their own face, from the front and from the side. This page shows what four rounds of tests produced, where it still fails, and what it takes to run it for every customer.

What was asked

  • GoalReplace the worn-by-model photos on a product page with photos of the customer wearing that frame, for any PID.
  • InputThe face scan Lenskart already takes, on a phone or on the store iPad, plus the PID's catalog images.
  • Must holdThe face is the customer's, and the frame is exactly the PID: shape, colour, rim, bridge, temples and lens tint.
  • Also askedCompare frontier models with open-source models, find the cost per image and the GPU need, and plan how it plugs into the existing stack.
  • Per customerAt least two images per frame: one from the front and one from the side.
  • Recognisable. Someone who knows the customer would say it is them.
  • Exact product. The frame matches the PID's own product photo.
  • Looks like a photo. Natural skin, no pasted look, no extra people or text.
  • Affordable at volume. A cost per customer that works across thousands of scans a day.
  • A machine checks it first. Every image is scored before a customer sees it; people review the edge cases.

Recommended setup

Three parts: a frontier model through its API, an in-house profile for every existing customer, and a clear wait message for new ones.

  1. 1 · Every customerGenerate with GPT Image 2.5 or Nano Banana ProBuy API credit for GPT Image 2.5 (first choice) or Nano Banana Pro. They gave the most faithful faces in every test. Each frame gets two images: front and side.
  2. 2 · Existing customersAn in-house profile, ready before they askWe already hold scans for most returning customers. Each is labelled an existing customer with a stored profile: best scan, face signature, face shape, skin tone and facial hair. When they scan again we recognise them, skip the checks and start from the profile, so their images can be made ahead of time and shown at once.
  3. 3 · New customersGenerate live, with a wait messageA first-time customer's images take about 45 seconds per frame. The screen shows "Your images are being generated, please wait" and swaps them in as they arrive. From then on they are an existing customer.

The same customer data trains our own LoRAs for an in-house model, which lowers cost as it matures. GPT Image 2.5 and Nano Banana Pro do not load custom LoRAs, so at launch the profile and the cache do the speeding up.

What the tests ran on

  • Store scans73,741 in-store face scans from September 2026, about 2,400 a day across 2,844 stores, each labelled for photo quality, age, gender, skin tone, face shape and facial hair.
  • Web scans36,819 face scans from the website and app over one week, about 5,300 a day. Smaller (480 px) and often blurry selfies.
  • FramesSix PIDs across Hustlr, John Jacobs and Meller: three eyeglasses and three sunglasses, each with worn-by-model shots.
  • CustomersUp to 12 store scans, chosen across gender, skin tone and facial hair. The head-to-head tests used two of them on two PIDs.

  5. Decision · 12 NovClosed models for launchGPT Image 2.5 first, Nano Banana Pro as backup. Open source parked until it is trained on far more data.

Best

Front and side, white studio, two customers

The customer's scan and the product photo go in. The shot is written in the prompt, the same for everyone: white background, head and shoulders, front or three-quarter turn, soft light, plain grey T-shirt.

Round 4

Locked studio pose across five models

Two customers × two PIDs × two poses. Moving the face into another model's pose was the hard part, and describing a neutral pose removed it. GPT Image 2.5 rose from 0.60 to 0.74, Seedream 5 Pro from 0.51 to 0.71, and Nano Banana Pro from 0.50 to 0.59 in a single call.

Round 3

Open-source models, shortcuts and a first LoRA

Qwen without training returned the catalog model unchanged. A local face swap gamed the face score. A LoRA trained on 20 good pairs from GPT Image 2.5 started to swap faces on wide shots but blended faces on close-ups.

Round 2

Ten ways to prompt the same swap

Identity-first prompts, a head crop of the scan, adding the product photo, a two-step edit (glasses first, then the catalog pose) and a corrective follow-up edit. Glasses added to the customer's own photo scored 0.76, but anything in the catalog pose stopped near 0.60.

How one image is made

Wiring

Six steps from a face scan to an image on the product page. The first steps run today as test scripts; the last two are designed but not built.

1 · Scan
Exists today

The face scan from the store iPad or the phone. A quality gate will reject blurry, dark or covered faces before anything is spent.

in face scan
out usable photo
2 · Prepare
Built (test)

Find the face, crop the head with hair and shoulders, enlarge it so the face fills the reference.

  • prep.pyhead crop and enlarge
3 · Prompt
Built (test)

One fixed studio prompt for everyone, filled with the PID's own attributes: shape, colour, lens.

  • studio.pyprompt and pose lock
4 · Generate
Built (test)

Front and side images in parallel through the model API: GPT Image 2.5, with Nano Banana Pro as backup.

  • falx.pymodel API calls
5 · Check
Partly built

Score every image: is it the customer, is it the exact frame, does it look real. Failures regenerate once, then go to the backup model.

  • qc.pyface match today; frame check planned
6 · Serve & cache
Designed

Store each image once. A returning customer who still looks the same gets their images instantly; a changed look regenerates.

out images on the product page

How the cache decides

  • Same person?The new scan's face is compared with the stored scan for that customer.
  • Same look?Beard, glasses, skin tone and hairstyle are compared, and stored scans expire after a set age.
  • Both yesShow the stored images at once, labelled with the scan date and a refresh option.
  • OtherwiseShow "generating" and replace the images as they arrive, about 45 seconds per frame.

The thresholds need real repeat scans to set. The pilot uses power customers (the most frequent buyers), who have several scans over time.

The pick: GPT Image 2.5 first, Nano Banana Pro as backup

GPT Image 2.5 Flare gave the most faithful face in every approach we tried (0.74 in the studio setup, 0.60 in the catalog pose) and kept small identity details. It costs about a third more than Nano Banana Pro and takes about 10 seconds longer.

Nano Banana Pro stays as the backup: images that fail the check on GPT Image 2.5 regenerate here, and it keeps the service running if one provider has an outage.

Ways to bring the cost down

  • Generate only what is looked at. Make images for the frames a customer opens or shortlists, not the whole catalog.
  • Cache by look. A returning customer whose look has not changed costs nothing.
  • Buy direct and in batch. Google lists Nano Banana Pro at about $0.134 per image direct, and about half that in batch mode for overnight pre-generation of power customers. To be confirmed with Google.
  • Try a lower quality setting. GPT Image 2.5 has cheaper quality levels; they have not been tested for face accuracy yet.
